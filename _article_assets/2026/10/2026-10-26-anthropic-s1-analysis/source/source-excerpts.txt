Customer Usage Patterns
Our revenue is influenced by customer usage patterns, which are impacted by the frequency and capability of
new model version releases, as well as seasonal trends, each of which can create variability in our results from
period to period. The continuous and overlapping cadence of new model version releases is inherent to
remaining at the frontier of AI development. For example, model versions released in the six months ended
September 30, 2026 accounted for over 80% of our revenue for the three months ended September 30, 2026.
As we have released successive versions of Claude models, we have continued to see revenue growth as new
capabilities unlock additional use cases, prompting new customers to adopt Claude and existing customers to
expand their usage.
Our customer usage patterns have also typically exhibited seasonality, particularly around holiday and vacation
periods in the summer and winter, when certain workflows slow down and overall customer activity declines.
For example, developer commits and feature launches are typically slower during the code freezes that many
companies schedule over holidays. Consistent with this pattern, for the two weeks ended January 2, 2026, total
customer spend declined by approximately 20% compared to the prior two weeks ended December 19, 2025.
However, our historical growth rates may mask the impact of seasonal trends on our results. These dynamics
are inherent aspects of a pay-for-intelligence revenue model and may be important determinants of our
financial results in future periods.
Because of these seasonal patterns, our results of operations have varied, and are expected to continue to vary,
from period to period, and our results for any one period may not be indicative of our results for any future
period. As our revenue growth rate declines over time, we expect seasonal effects to become more apparent in,
and to have a greater effect on, our period-to-period results, and to make it more difficult to forecast our results
of operations.

Operating Expenses
Compute and Infrastructure
Compute and infrastructure expenses primarily consist of costs attributable to operating our technology
infrastructure, which supports the full range of workloads for our models, including research, model
development, serving customers, and internal usage of Claude. These costs primarily consist of compute fees
paid to CSPs under committed and on-demand arrangements, including storage, networking, and connectivity,
and related support costs, infrastructure costs associated with serving customer workloads through partner
channels, colocation facility costs, equipment leases and committed power costs associated with our data center
operations, and third-party application infrastructure software costs directly related to our compute operations.
Beginning in 2026, as our obligations commence under compute and infrastructure leases, these expenses also
include operating lease costs, amortization of finance lease assets, variable lease costs, and depreciation and
amortization on owned servers, networking equipment, and related data center assets. See Note 8 to our
consolidated financial statements included elsewhere in this prospectus for a functional presentation of our
operating expenses, including cost of revenue, and a description of the workloads underlying the compute and
infrastructure expenses within each functional caption. Within that functional presentation, substantially all of
the cost of revenue consists of compute and infrastructure expenses associated with customer-serving
workloads. Additionally, cost of revenue includes certain personnel-related costs and allocated overhead costs.
Customers adopt and retain our technology based on whether our models are at the frontier of AI capability and
maintaining that frontier position is essential to revenue generation. Each model release represents continuous
intelligence improvements, making ongoing model development a critical factor in revenue generation. We
operate an iterative cycle in which compute deployed to model development yields more capable and efficient
models. Those efficiency gains free compute to serve additional customer workloads. In turn, serving our
customers at scale generates feedback that informs research and model development. In this cycle, customer-
serving activity and model development are each a direct input that informs the other, and we therefore view
compute and infrastructure costs as critical to our ability to generate revenue within a short time of incurring
such costs.
We expect compute and infrastructure expenses to increase in absolute dollars and remain our largest
operating expense for the foreseeable future as additional committed compute becomes available to us under
multi-year arrangements with CSPs, and as we continue to invest in additional compute. These expenses are
paid and recognized as compute becomes available to us, rather than when committed, and are generally fixed
in the near term given the majority of our compute is contracted under multi-year commitments that we
typically procure 12 to 18 months in advance of availability. We deploy this committed compute as a single,
shared resource across all workloads, and thus plan, incur, and assess it on a total basis rather than by
individual workload. As a result, compute and infrastructure expenses as a percentage of revenue will depend
primarily on how our revenue scales relative to our total committed compute cost base. In addition, as part of
our public benefit initiatives, we intend to direct a meaningful portion of our resources, which may include
compute, toward ensuring that the benefits of frontier AI are broadly accessible, and we expect to significantly
scale these efforts over time.
